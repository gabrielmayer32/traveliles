**Homes illuminated with lights, homemade sweets and doors open to friends and neighbours… In Mauritius, Diwali goes far beyond its religious significance. It is a celebration of generosity and togetherness, where traditions passed down through generations bring families, friends and communities closer.**

Celebrated by the Hindu community, Diwali also called Divali or Dipavali, the Festival of Lights, symbolises the triumph of light over darkness and good over evil. Homes are illuminated in honour of Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity, to welcome divine blessings.

Beyond its spiritual significance, however, the festival celebrated on the 8th of November this year, holds a particularly special place in Mauritian life. As the people we met during the celebrations remind us, opening one's home is also about welcoming others, regardless of their background or beliefs.

**The joy of giving and receiving**

In kitchens across the island, preparing traditional Diwali sweets is an essential part of the festivities. Families make delicacies to share with relatives, friends and neighbours, who in turn offer their own homemade treats.

Children also eagerly take part in this tradition, going from house to house to share sweets with their friends. Simple gestures that create lasting childhood memories and keep a rich cultural heritage alive from one generation to the next.

**A light that brings people together**

On an island where so many cultures and beliefs coexist, Diwali reflects the Mauritian spirit of embracing and sharing one another's traditions. Lights extend beyond the walls of family homes, sweets are exchanged and the joy of being together transcends differences.

Through this video, Travel-Îles invites you to experience Diwali from within, through heartfelt stories that reveal the warmth, generosity and humanity of Mauritius.

A celebration that reminds us that light shines brightest when it is shared.
