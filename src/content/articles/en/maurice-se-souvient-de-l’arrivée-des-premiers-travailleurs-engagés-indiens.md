Just a short walk from the bustling streets of Port Louis, overlooking the harbour, a few weathered stone steps tell a story that has shaped Mauritius for generations. As the island prepares to mark the arrival of indentured labourers on 2 November, Aapravasi Ghat stands as a powerful reminder of the people and journeys that helped define the nation.

**A Turning Point in the Island’s History**

On 2 November 1834, the _Atlas_ arrived in Mauritius carrying the first indentured labourers from India. Their arrival marked the beginning of a major wave of migration that would profoundly transform the island.

![](/images/uploads/TI%20Aapravasi%20Ghat%20%282%29.webp)

Over the following decades, nearly half a million indentured workers made the journey to Mauritius, most of them to work on sugar plantations. Behind these numbers were men and women who had left their homelands behind, often facing harsh living and working conditions. They also brought languages, traditions and skills that would become an integral part of Mauritian life.

**A Legacy That Lives On**

Built in 1849, the immigration depot at Aapravasi Ghat served as a gateway for new arrivals before they were sent to work on sugar estates. Today, its surviving remains, recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2006, preserve the memory of this defining chapter in the island’s past.

![](/images/uploads/TI%20Aapravasi%20Ghat%20%283%29.webp)

Yet its legacy reaches far beyond the historic site. It lives on in Mauritius’ food, festivals, traditions and the many cultural influences that make the island what it is today.

Every 2 November offers an opportunity to honour these generations who, alongside the island’s other communities, helped shape modern Mauritius. A story worth remembering, sharing and exploring through a visit to one of Port Louis’ most meaningful heritage sites.
