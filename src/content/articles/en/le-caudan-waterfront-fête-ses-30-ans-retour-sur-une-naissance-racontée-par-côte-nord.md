In 1996, Port Louis welcomed a new destination that would transform its waterfront and become one of the capital's most recognisable landmarks. Thirty years on, Caudan Waterfront holds a special place in the memories of generations of Mauritians. And from the very beginning, Côte Nord magazine was there to capture the story.

**1996: A New Chapter for Port Louis**

In October 1996, issue 21 of Côte Nord dedicated a double-page feature to the opening of Caudan Waterfront, with a headline that captured the excitement of the moment: “Le Caudan Waterfront ou la renaissance de Port-Louis” — Caudan Waterfront and the Rebirth of Port Louis.

The development represented a turning point for the capital. Former port land had been transformed into a lively destination, bringing together shops, restaurants, entertainment and waterfront promenades. It offered Mauritians a completely new way to experience Port Louis, beyond its traditional role as the island's commercial and administrative centre.

The magazine enthusiastically introduced readers to Barkly Wharf, the craft market, boutiques, terraces and restaurants. From the outset, Caudan was conceived as more than a shopping centre. It was somewhere to stroll, meet friends, enjoy a meal and rediscover the city from a different perspective.

**December 1996: Caudan Takes Centre Stage**

Just two months later, Caudan Waterfront appeared on the cover of Côte Nord's December issue, number 22, dressed for the festive season. A photograph of its distinctive new architecture accompanied the greeting: “Joyeuses fêtes au Caudan Waterfront”.

![](/images/uploads/Couverture%20CN%20no%2022%20Decembre%201996_page-0001.webp)

Inside, a special feature captured the excitement of those early days: newly opened restaurants, international brands, local craftspeople showcasing their skills and the cosmopolitan atmosphere beginning to take shape along the harbour.

The headlines reflected this diversity: “L'artisanat en fête” (A Celebration of Craftsmanship), “Des huiles, des steaks et des monstres” (Oils, Steaks and Monsters), and “Un lieu universel” (A Place for Everyone).

Together, these stories painted a picture of a destination finding its identity, where food, art, shopping and entertainment came together. It was a new expression of Mauritius: modern and outward-looking, yet proud to celebrate local creativity and traditions.

**Thirty Years of Stories and Memories**

Today, revisiting these pages feels like opening a window onto another era. They take us back to a time when everything was new, when the first shops were welcoming their customers and Mauritians were discovering a destination that would soon become part of everyday life.

![](/images/uploads/Dossier%20Caudan%20CN%20No%2022%20_page-0001.webp)

Over the years, Caudan has welcomed generations of families, visitors, artists and city wanderers. For many, it brings back memories of weekend outings, lunches overlooking the harbour, leisurely walks or simply the pleasure of experiencing the energy of Port Louis.

For Travel-Îles, these archives also celebrate the legacy of Côte Nord, a magazine that documented Mauritius as it evolved, capturing the places, people and initiatives shaping the island's identity.

Thirty years later, these early features tell a shared story: that of a waterfront destination that became a Mauritian landmark, and a magazine that was there to document its very first chapter.
