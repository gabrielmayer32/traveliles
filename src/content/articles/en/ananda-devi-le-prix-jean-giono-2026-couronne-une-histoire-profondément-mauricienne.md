On 10 November 2026, acclaimed Mauritian author Ananda Devi was awarded the prestigious Prix Jean Giono in Paris for her latest novel, _Chronique d’un royaume perdu_, published by Grasset. The award celebrates not only one of the most distinctive voices in French-language literature, but also a story deeply connected to Mauritius, its history and its cultural heritage.

Published in August, the novel takes readers to Le Bouchon, on the island’s south-east coast, where a small community struggles to build a life of its own, away from the violence and divisions left by slavery. Spanning four generations, the story weaves together family destinies, secrets, love and beliefs against the backdrop of Mauritius.

Blending reality with elements of the supernatural, Ananda Devi creates a world shaped by legends, mysteries and memories. Nature plays a central role, as does the island’s rich cultural heritage, influenced by African, Indian and European traditions.

**A Story Forty Years in the Making**

Ananda Devi has revealed that this novel had been taking shape in her mind for nearly four decades. She describes it as a love letter to Mauritius, its stories and its multicultural identity.

Born in Trois-Boutiques in 1957, the author has spent decades exploring the many layers of Mauritian society, its complexities, struggles and humanity. Her work has been translated into numerous languages and has earned her international recognition, including the prestigious Neustadt International Prize for Literature in 2024.

Established in 1990, the Prix Jean Giono honours a French-language literary work each year, celebrating the power of imagination and storytelling.

With _Chronique d’un royaume perdu_, Ananda Devi reminds us that Mauritius is more than a place of remarkable landscapes. It is also an island of stories, memories and imagination, whose voices can resonate far beyond its shores.
