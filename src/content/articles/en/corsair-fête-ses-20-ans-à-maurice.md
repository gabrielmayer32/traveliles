**From the Boeing 747 in 2006 to today's Airbus A330neo, two decades of connecting France and Mauritius.**

On 25 October 2006, a Boeing 747 bearing Corsair's tricolour livery touched down at Plaisance Airport for the first time. Twenty years later, the airline celebrated this milestone in Mauritius, with up to six weekly flights this winter from Paris-Orly, Marseille and Toulouse, alongside a renewed partnership with the island's tourism promotion authority.

**An anniversary celebration at Labourdonnais**

On 19 November, Corsair brought together its partners at the Château de Labourdonnais in Mapou to celebrate two decades of operations in Mauritius. The airline's Chairman and CEO, Pascal de Izaguirre, travelled to the island for the occasion. Among the guests were the French Ambassador to Mauritius, Alexis Loyer, representatives of the Ministry of Tourism and travel industry professionals.

Much has changed over the past twenty years. In 2006, Corsair operated two weekly flights. A decade later, that number had doubled, with more than 100,000 passengers travelling to the island each year. For the 2026–2027 winter season, the airline has announced up to six weekly flights, five of them direct.

**2006: Opening up Mauritius' skies**

The arrival of Corsair, then known as Corsairfly and part of the TUI Group, owed much to the open-skies policy championed by Xavier-Luc Duval, Mauritius' Minister of Tourism at the time.

Until then, air travel between France and Mauritius had largely been dominated by Air France and Air Mauritius. The newcomer sought to complement the existing services: “We want to offer greater capacity and attractive fares,” the airline explained in _Côte Nord_ in December 2006.

Depending on the season, the first flights were operated by either a 582-seat Boeing 747-400 or an Airbus A330-200. For Mauritian hoteliers, the arrival of Corsair offered fresh momentum for the French market, which had been affected that year by the chikungunya outbreak on neighbouring Réunion Island.

**Three departure cities in France**

Paris-Orly remains Corsair's historic gateway to Mauritius, with a direct flight time of approximately eleven hours. Marseille also features among the departure cities this winter, while Toulouse is connected to Mauritius via Réunion Island, a route announced in February 2026.

“Mauritius enhances the long-haul network we are developing from France's regional cities,” Pascal de Izaguirre explained at the time.

For travellers from other cities, Corsair's Train + Air service provides connections to Paris-Orly via Massy railway station, with high-speed rail services from 21 French cities and Brussels.

The airline's fleet has also undergone a significant transformation. Corsair now operates nine Airbus A330neo aircraft, offering three cabin classes: Business, Premium and Economy. Business Class features fully flat beds, a wine selection curated by award-winning French sommelier Antoine Pétrus, holder of the prestigious _Meilleur Ouvrier de France_ title, and amenity kits by French skincare brand Sothys.

**A renewed partnership with the MTPA**

In September, during the IFTM Top Resa travel trade show in Paris, Corsair and the Mauritius Tourism Promotion Authority (MTPA) renewed their partnership, first established in 2006.

The agreement includes several joint promotional campaigns targeting both consumers and travel industry professionals. One such campaign runs from 16 to 29 November across the Greater Paris region, featuring advertising on the Paris Métro and online.

For Mauritius, this milestone represents more than an anniversary. In 2006, Corsair's arrival marked a turning point in the opening of the island's skies to new airlines. Twenty years on, with three departure cities and a modernised fleet, Corsair is reaffirming its long-standing commitment to the destination.

**From our archives: Corsair's arrival in Mauritius**

**Côte Nord No. 74 – September 2006**

Even before its inaugural flight, Corsairfly made the cover of _Côte Nord_, featuring a Boeing 747 taking off under the headline “Corsairfly à Maurice”. The issue included an interview exploring the launch of the Paris–Lyon–Mauritius route, described as “the culmination of lengthy discussions”.

**Côte Nord No. 75 – December 2006**

It took “more than ten years of discussions and two years of intense negotiations” before Corsairfly's Boeing 747-400 finally touched down in Mauritius.

On board the inaugural flight were 474 passengers, including 450 fare-paying travellers, as well as two Mauritian cabin crew members. Two weekly flights were initially scheduled: Tuesdays via Lyon and Sundays direct from Paris-Orly.

The magazine also acknowledged the contribution of Philippe Hitié, whom Jean-Marc Siano, Chairman of Corsairfly's Executive Board, referred to as “our ambassador in Mauritius”.

**Côte Nord No. 77 – June 2007**

_Côte Nord_ experienced Corsairfly's Grand Large cabin aboard the Boeing 747 on a flight from Mauritius to Paris. In the airline's in-flight magazine, CEO Hervé Pierret wrote: “Among our new scheduled destinations, Mauritius, launched in late 2006, has proved particularly popular with our passengers.”

**Côte Nord No. 125 – November 2016**

Corsair celebrated its tenth anniversary in Mauritius with four weekly flights, more than 100,000 passengers a year and a load factor of 85% over the previous three years. Pascal de Izaguirre also announced the introduction of Business Class for summer 2017.
